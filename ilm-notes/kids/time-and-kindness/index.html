Notes for parents

About 15 minutes, then a week of the mission. Read each Ask them first box and wait for an answer before anyone taps.

  1. Open with the 24 coins. Let them argue about saving, then reveal it's hours.
  2. In “By time”, let each child plan their own day, then compare.
  3. At “Which surah?”, recite both surahs together before sorting.
  4. Pick one real thing for orphans or the poor this week, and do it together.
  5. Open the mission page each night for a week and tick together.

So you are not caught out: you may hear that patting an orphan's head earns a good deed for every hair. That narration is da'if, so it is left out here. And Imam ash-Shafi'i's famous line, that if people reflected on Al-'Asr alone it would be enough for them, is a scholar's saying, not a hadith.

What a good deed needs: sincerity for Allah, and following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. That's why the prayer chapter asks “for whom”, not just “did you”.

Pitching it by age

AgeAim forA question to ask
Around age 4–6One idea: be kind with little things, and share. Do the door, the fingers and the mission.“What can you share with your brother or sister today?”
Around age 7–9Time can't come back, and you need all four keys. The sorting game ties the two surahs together.“Which key is hardest for you?”
Around age 10–12The warning in Al-Ma'un is for people who do pray. Not believing in the akhirah shows in how we treat people. Read 63:10 together: people ask for more time, and it's refused.“If you knew today was your last day, what would you change?”
