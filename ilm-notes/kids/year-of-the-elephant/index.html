Notes for parents

About 15 minutes, one sitting. You tell the story, they do the tapping. In every chapter, read the Ask them first box and wait for an answer before anyone taps. The waiting is where the lesson lands.

  1. Open with the question on the cover and let them argue about it.
  2. Go chapter by chapter. Let a different child hold the screen each time.
  3. At the surah, recite it together before the matching game.
  4. End with My Elephant. Each child picks their own, out loud.
  5. Later that day, ask one of them to retell it to someone who was not there.

Three places where the reports differ, so you are not caught out. The number of elephants: one, eight or twelve. What happened to the church: one man dirtied it, or in another report some young men's fire burnt it. And Abraha's return: the reports I checked say his men carried him while his body fell apart piece by piece, not that he crawled. If you have heard that he crawled, it is worth asking which book that is from.

Pitching it by age

AgeAim forA question to ask
Around age 4–6One idea only: Allah is stronger than anything big. Let them do the building, the turning and the birds. Read chapter 7 gently and keep it short.“Who looked after the Ka'bah when everyone went up the mountain?”
Around age 7–9Retelling the story in order, and matching each ayah to its part.“What was Abdul Muttalib's job, and what did he leave to Allah?”
Around age 10–12The harder thought behind it. Ibn Kathir points out that Abraha's people had a revealed book while Quraysh then worshipped idols. Makkah was not saved because Quraysh deserved it. Allah was protecting His House, and preparing the city for the Prophet ﷺ.“Read the second half of 3:160. What makes a person lose Allah's help? What did Abraha do that we should watch for in ourselves?”
