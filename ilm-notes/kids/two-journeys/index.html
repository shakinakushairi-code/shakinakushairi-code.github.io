Notes for parents

About 15 minutes, one sitting. You tell the story, they do the tapping. In every chapter, read the Ask them first box and wait for an answer before anyone taps.

  1. Open with the cover question and let them guess.
  2. Go chapter by chapter. Let a different child hold the screen each time.
  3. At the surah, recite it together before the matching game.
  4. End with the thank-you list. Each child says their three out loud.
  5. At dinner that night, ask: “Who fed us today?”

One difference of opinion, so you are not caught out: what the first word li- (“for…”) connects to. Some scholars link it to Surah Al-Fil (Allah destroyed the army for Quraysh's safety), some to ayah 3 (because of this gift, worship Him), and at-Tabari reads it as wonder (“marvel at the habit of Quraysh”). All are valid views.

Pitching it by age

AgeAim forA question to ask
Around age 4–6One idea: Allah gives us our food and keeps us safe, so we say thank you to Him.“Who gave us our dinner?”
Around age 7–9The story in order, and matching each ayah to its part. Link it to the Year of the Elephant.“Why did the robbers leave Quraysh alone?”
Around age 10–12Allah asked for one thing in return: worship. Most of Quraysh enjoyed the gifts and still worshipped idols. Having nikmat isn't the test; what we do with it is.“What gifts do we enjoy every day but forget to thank Allah for?”
