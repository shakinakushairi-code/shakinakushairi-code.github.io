Al-Falaq & An-Nas · 113–114The Bedtime Shield

Notes for parents

About 15 minutes. Best done at bedtime, so the last chapter becomes the real routine. Read each Ask them first box and wait for an answer before anyone taps.

  1. Open with the cover question. Let them name what scares them, without brushing it off.
  2. Do the nightfall chapter while actually closing a door and covering a jug, if you can.
  3. At “Which surah?”, recite both surahs together before sorting.
  4. End in bed: do the last chapter for real, three rounds.
  5. Next night, see if they remember the steps without the screen.

Reading the three Qul three times every morning and evening is from Abu Dawud 5082 and at-Tirmidhi 3575. It is graded hasan (accepted, a level below sahih). I kept it out of the story and put it here so you can decide.

For the older ones only, and your call: the Prophet ﷺ himself was once affected by magic, and Allah revealed what it was so it could be removed (Sahih al-Bukhari 5763; Sahih Muslim 2189). It shows these surahs are protection, not a story. The lesson to stress: stay away from fortune-tellers and charms. Whoever goes to a fortune-teller and asks him about anything, his prayer is not accepted for forty nights (Sahih Muslim 2230).

Around age 4–6One idea: Allah keeps us safe at night. Do the dawn, the nightfall routine and the bedtime steps. Skip magic.“Who looks after us while we sleep?”
Around age 7–9Outside harm (Falaq) and inside whisper (Nas). The sorting game makes the difference stick.“What does the whisper tell you to do sometimes?”
Around age 10–12Hasad and the whisper both start in the heart, and the whisper can only invite. We are responsible for the choice (14:22).“When did you last feel jealous? What could you have said instead?”
