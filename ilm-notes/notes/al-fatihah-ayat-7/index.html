Surah Al-Fatihah: Ayat 7

Siratal-ladhina an'amta 'alaihim  |  Three paths, two ways to fail
In short
  • Those given nikmat combine knowledge and action.
  • Two failures: knowing but not doing, and doing without knowing.
  • Don't trick your way around the syariat — and don't stay silent.

2. Who were given nikmat? — An-Nisa' 4:69

What they all share: knowledge and action. That's the key to the whole ayat.

3. Three paths, two ways to fail

With knowledgeWithout knowledge
Acting✅ An'amta 'alaihim❌ Ad-Dallin — astray
Not acting❌ Al-Maghdub — earned anger❌
  • Al-Maghdub — knew the truth but hid it or didn't act on it (Al-Baqarah 2:159: those who hide what Allah revealed are cursed).
  • Ad-Dallin — act without knowledge, following desire, assumption, custom or the majority — without dalil.

لِيَحْمِلُوا أَوْزَارَهُمْ كَامِلَةً يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ ۙ وَمِنْ أَوْزَارِ الَّذِينَ يُضِلُّونَهُم بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ

"(Mereka) akan memikul dosa-dosa mereka sepenuhnya pada hari kiamat, dan sebahagian dosa orang-orang yang mereka sesatkan tanpa ilmu."

An-Nahl 16:25

4. The greatest nikmat

Not wealth, health, position or children — but Islam, iman and hidayah: "Pada hari ini telah Aku sempurnakan bagi kamu agama kamu, dan telah Aku cukupkan nikmat-Ku kepada kamu, dan Aku redha Islam menjadi agama kamu" (Al-Ma'idah 5:3).

5. Examples of those given nikmat

Abu Bakr r.a. — giving everything

The Prophet ﷺ told us to give sedekah. Umar r.a. thought: today I'll beat Abu Bakr — and brought half his wealth. Abu Bakr brought everything he had. The Prophet ﷺ asked what he left for his family. He said: "Aku tinggalkan untuk mereka Allah dan Rasul-Nya." Umar said: I'll never beat him in anything.

Abu Dawud 1678; Tirmidhi 3675 (Umar) · Hasan

Al-Baqarah 2:261 — those who spend in Allah's way are like a seed that grows seven ears, each with a hundred grains: 700 times — and Allah multiplies for whom He wills.

The privileges of the syahid

The hadith says six privileges (سِتُّ خِصَالٍ), which when spelled out come to seven items:

  1. Forgiven with the first drop of blood, and shown his place in Jannah
  2. Protected from the punishment of the grave
  3. Safe from the Great Terror
  4. A crown of dignity placed on his head — one ruby of it better than the world and all in it
  5. Married to 72 of the hur al-'ayn
  6. Allowed to intercede for 70 of his relatives

Tirmidhi 1663 (al-Miqdam bin Ma'dikarib) · Hasan sahih (at-Tirmidhi)

But niat decides everything. The first three thrown into Hell include a man who fought so people would call him brave, a scholar who learned so people would call him learned, and a man who gave so people would call him generous (Muslim 1905).

6. The Sabbath-breakers — Al-A'raf 7:163–166

A town was tested with a ban on fishing on the Sabbath. Three groups:

  1. Didn't break it and advised others → saved
  2. Didn't break it but didn't advise → "Why do you advise a people Allah will destroy?" (164)
  3. Broke it using a trick (setting nets on Friday, collecting on Sunday) → destroyed
Lesson: don't look for tricks to get around the syariat; and keep doing amar makruf nahi mungkar. The second group stayed quiet — and the Quran doesn't explicitly say they were saved.

Reflections

  • Two failures to watch for in ourselves. Maghdub — knowing what's right and not doing it. Dallin — doing things by habit, custom or "everyone does it" without checking the dalil. Most of us slip into both in different areas of life.
  • Abu Bakr gave everything — while alive. In life, there's no limit on what we can give. After death, a wasiat is limited to a third. If we want to give generously, the time is now.
  • The trick is the trap. The Sabbath-breakers didn't deny the rule — they found a clever way around it. Following the letter while defeating the purpose is its own kind of disobedience.
