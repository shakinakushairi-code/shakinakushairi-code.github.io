Class Notes · Tadabbur Siri 11

Surah Al-Falaq

Thursday, 20 August 2026  |  Surah ke-113 · 5 ayat  |  Protection from the evil that comes from outside
In short
  • Seek refuge in the Lord of the dawn from evil outside us: the night, sihir, hasad.
  • Read the 3 Qul morning, evening and before sleep, as the Prophet ﷺ did.
  • Stay away from fortune-tellers; real protection is tawakkal and zikir.
Why tadabbur? "Tidakkah mereka mentadabbur Al-Quran, atau adakah hati mereka telah terkunci?" (Muhammad 47:24). Allah describes refusing to reflect on the Quran as a sign of a locked heart — a rebuke, not just a suggestion.

2. 3 Qul — morning, evening, and before sleep

Abdullah bin Khubaib r.a.: the Prophet ﷺ told him to read Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas three times in the morning and evening — "it will suffice you against everything" (Abu Dawud 5082; Tirmidhi 3575 · hasan). Hasan, not sahih — but still accepted and widely practised in daily zikir.

Every night before sleeping, the Prophet ﷺ would cup his hands together, blow into them, recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, then wipe over as much of his body as he could — starting with his head and face — three times.

Bukhari 5017 (Aisyah) · Sahih

3. When the Prophet ﷺ was affected by sihir

Aisyah r.a. narrated that the Prophet ﷺ was affected by sihir from Labid bin al-A'sam of Bani Zuraiq — it made him feel he had done something he hadn't. Allah informed him of its cause: hair knotted in a comb, hidden in a well, and it was removed (Bukhari 5763; Muslim 2189). This is the direct link to why Al-Falaq and An-Nas matter as protection.

4. Ayat by ayat

Ayat 1 — the Lord of Al-Falaq

  • Qul — Allah teaches the Prophet ﷺ and us to seek protection from Him alone.
  • A'udhu — not just asking for help, but running to Him for refuge.
  • Bi-Rabbi — Allah as Rabb: Creator, Owner, Manager, Protector.
  • Al-Falaq — dawn; something that splits open — light breaking through darkness. Night turning into day shows He creates everything.

Ayat 2 — the evil of what He created

Allah allows evil to exist (dunia is a test, with good and bad) — but He is not pleased with it. Examples from class: sihir, zina. Yusuf 12:53 — "Sesungguhnya nafsu itu sangat menyuruh kepada kejahatan, kecuali nafsu yang diberi rahmat oleh Tuhanku."

Ayat 3 — the evil of the night

Night is often when evil spreads — clubs, zina, theft, even pests and disease (scorpions, mosquitoes).

When night begins to fall, keep your children in, because the shaitan spread out at that time. Close the doors, put out the lamps, tie your waterskins and cover your containers — mentioning the name of Allah.

Bukhari 3304; Muslim 2012–2013 (Jabir) · Sahih

Ayat 4 — those who blow on knots

Traditional sihir was done by blowing on knots in a string — the class noted it was often done by women in the past. Directly linked to the sihir of Labid above.

Ayat 5 — the evil of hasad

Two forms of hasad: (1) wanting someone's nikmat to become yours; (2) wanting their nikmat to disappear even if you don't get it — the second is clearly the blameworthy kind.

"Tidak ada hasad (yang dibenarkan) kecuali dalam dua perkara: seseorang yang Allah beri harta lalu dia membelanjakannya pada kebenaran, dan seseorang yang Allah beri hikmah lalu dia mengamalkan dan mengajarkannya."

Bukhari 73; Muslim 816 (Ibn Mas'ud) · Sahih

This "permitted hasad" is really ghibtah — healthy aspiration to do the same good, without wishing the other person loses anything.

5. Fortune-tellers, ruqyah and tawakkal

  • Fortune-tellers: whoever asks an 'arraf about something, his solat isn't accepted for 40 nights (Muslim 2230) — this includes believing in Chinese zodiac years, horoscopes, and palm reading.
  • The 70,000 who enter Jannah without hisab: they don't ask others for ruqyah, don't believe in bad omens (tatayyur), don't use cauterisation — and they put their trust in their Lord (Bukhari 5705; Muslim 220).
  • At-Talaq 65:2–3 — whoever has taqwa, Allah makes a way out for him and provides from where he never expected.

Summary

Seek refuge in Allah from the evil of the night, sihir and hasad; return to tawakkal, taqwa, zikir and ruqyah through the 3 Qul — read in the morning, evening, and before sleep.

Reflections

  • The quiet hours need the most guarding. The Prophet ﷺ told us to shut doors and cover containers exactly when things go quiet and dark. The same applies to us: the moments no one is watching are where we need the most discipline.
  • Hasad within families often isn't about how much someone got, but about comparing with what others got. Being open and clear about our intentions while we're alive can leave less room for hasad to grow later.
