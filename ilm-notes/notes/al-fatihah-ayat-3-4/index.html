Surah Al-Fatihah: Ayat 3–4

Ar-Rahmanir-Rahim · Maliki yawmid-din  |  Mercy, then the Day of Judgement
In short
  • Allah's power is built on mercy; feeling 'enough' is what makes us transgress.
  • On the Day of Judgement, ownership returns to the true Owner.
  • Harta is the only thing asked about twice: where from, and where to.

Part A — Ayat 3: Ar-Rahmanir-Rahim

Why is rahmat mentioned again?

Right after praising Himself as Rabbil-'alamin (absolute power), Allah immediately mentions Ar-Rahman Ar-Rahim. The order is deliberate: even though He rules everything, His power is built on mercy, not harshness.

Ar-Rahman vs Ar-Rahim

الرَّحْمَٰن · Ar-Rahmanالرَّحِيم · Ar-Rahim
TypeA sifat (attribute) of AllahAn action of Allah
ScopeCovers all creation — believer or notSpecial mercy for the believers
Points toWho Allah isWhat Allah does for the believers

Examples of the special mercy: the gift of iman, hidayah, beneficial ilmu, ease in worship, peace of heart, and Jannah.

We forget ourselves when we feel "enough"

كَلَّا إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَيَطْغَىٰ ۝ أَن رَّآهُ اسْتَغْنَىٰ

"Ingatlah! Sesungguhnya manusia benar-benar melampaui batas, apabila dia melihat dirinya telah cukup (serba-serbi)."

Al-'Alaq 96:6–7

Notice the trigger: not hardship, but istighna' — feeling self-sufficient. Give us wealth, power or status, and we easily become riak, sombong, think we're above others. Allah is the opposite.

The heart finds peace in remembering Allah

أَلَا بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ الْقُلُوبُ

"Ketahuilah, dengan mengingati Allah, hati menjadi tenang."

Ar-Ra'd 13:28

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ، وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ

"Sesungguhnya Allah tidak melihat kepada rupa dan harta kamu, tetapi Dia melihat kepada hati dan amalan kamu."

Muslim 2564 (Abu Hurairah) · Sahih

Real ketenangan is the kind that brings us closer to Allah — and the only road to it is zikrullah.

From class: speak gently, even to Firaun

فَقُولَا لَهُ قَوْلًا لَّيِّنًا لَّعَلَّهُ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَىٰ

"Maka berkatalah kamu berdua kepadanya dengan kata-kata yang lemah lembut, mudah-mudahan dia ingat atau takut."

Ta-Ha 20:44

Allah told Musa and Harun a.s. to speak gently — to Firaun, the worst tyrant. If even he got qawlan layyinan, there's no excuse for harshness in everyday dakwah.

Part B — Ayat 4: Maliki yawmid-din

Two authentic qira'at

BacaanMakna
مَالِكِ — MālikiPemilik (Owner) of the Day of Judgement
مَلِكِ — MalikiRaja (King) of the Day of Judgement

Both are sahih and mutawatir from the Prophet ﷺ — not a contradiction, but two layers of meaning: ownership (milk) and sovereignty (mulk). Ad-Din here = the day everyone gets the balasan for their deeds.

Why remind us of that Day?

Because we're so easily lalai with dunia. On that Day there's no more kezaliman, penipuan, rasuah, or "orang dalam".

يَوْمَ هُم بَارِزُونَ ۖ لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى اللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَيْءٌ ۚ لِّمَنِ الْمُلْكُ الْيَوْمَ ۖ لِلَّهِ الْوَاحِدِ الْقَهَّارِ

"(Iaitu) hari mereka keluar (dari kubur); tiada sesuatu pun keadaan mereka tersembunyi daripada Allah. (Allah berfirman): Milik siapakah kerajaan pada hari ini? Milik Allah, Yang Maha Esa lagi Maha Perkasa."

Ghafir 40:16

A question Allah answers Himself — because there's no one left to answer it.

Friends become enemies

الْأَخِلَّاءُ يَوْمَئِذٍ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ إِلَّا الْمُتَّقِينَ

"Sahabat-sahabat karib pada hari itu menjadi musuh antara satu sama lain, kecuali orang-orang yang bertakwa."

Az-Zukhruf 43:67

Taqwa = doing what Allah commands and leaving what He forbids.

The questions that hold our feet

لَا تَزُولُ قَدَمَا عَبْدٍ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ حَتَّى يُسْأَلَ عَنْ عُمُرِهِ فِيمَا أَفْنَاهُ، وَعَنْ عِلْمِهِ فِيمَ فَعَلَ، وَعَنْ مَالِهِ مِنْ أَيْنَ اكْتَسَبَهُ وَفِيمَ أَنْفَقَهُ، وَعَنْ جِسْمِهِ فِيمَ أَبْلَاهُ

"Tidak akan berganjak kedua kaki seorang hamba pada hari kiamat sehingga dia ditanya tentang umurnya — ke mana dihabiskan; ilmunya — apa yang diamalkan; hartanya — dari mana diperoleh dan ke mana dibelanjakan; dan tubuh badannya — ke mana digunakan."

Tirmidhi 2417 (Abu Barzah al-Aslami) · Hasan sahih

The version mentioned in class with "masa muda" is a similar narration from Ibn Mas'ud (Tirmidhi 2416), graded hasan by al-Albani.

Notice: harta is the only thing asked about twice — where it came from and where it went.

That long, terrifying Day is so for those who don't receive Allah's rahmat.

Dunia is not our goal

Allah owns dunia — and He gives it even to His enemies. So why are we so busy chasing dunia that we forget its Owner? The Prophet ﷺ said he didn't fear poverty for us, but that dunia would be spread out for us and we'd compete for it and be destroyed by it (Bukhari 3158; Muslim 2961).

يَدْخُلُ فُقَرَاءُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ الْجَنَّةَ قَبْلَ أَغْنِيَائِهِمْ بِنِصْفِ يَوْمٍ، وَهُوَ خَمْسُمِائَةِ عَامٍ

"Orang-orang fakir dalam kalangan Muslimin masuk syurga sebelum orang-orang kaya mereka dengan setengah hari, iaitu lima ratus tahun."

Tirmidhi 2354 · Ibn Majah 4122 · Hasan sahih

Not because poverty itself is noble — but because their hisab is lighter. Less harta, fewer questions.

Seven under the shade of Allah

On the Day there's no shade but His, seven groups are shaded (Bukhari 660; Muslim 1031):

  1. A just leader
  2. A youth who grew up worshipping Allah
  3. A man whose heart is attached to the masjid
  4. Two people who love each other for Allah, meeting and parting for Him
  5. A man invited by a woman of status and beauty, who says "I fear Allah"
  6. One who gives sedekah so secretly that the left hand doesn't know what the right gave
  7. One who remembers Allah alone and his eyes flow with tears

Many ulama understand "seven" as not an absolute limit, since other narrations mention more groups.

Sedekah in secret

إِن تُبْدُوا الصَّدَقَاتِ فَنِعِمَّا هِيَ ۖ وَإِن تُخْفُوهَا وَتُؤْتُوهَا الْفُقَرَاءَ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ

"Jika kamu menampakkan sedekah, maka itu baik; dan jika kamu menyembunyikannya dan memberikannya kepada orang fakir, maka itu lebih baik bagi kamu."

Al-Baqarah 2:271

Allah already knows. No need to tell people — unless there's a real maslahat. The ayat doesn't condemn open giving; it just ranks the hidden kind higher.

Reflections

  • Feeling "enough" is the real risk. 96:6–7 names the trigger precisely: not hardship, but istighna'. While there's still need, du'a stays alive. Once everything's covered, du'a can quietly become a ritual.
  • Allah doesn't look at net worth (Muslim 2564). A little that's carefully halal is in a better place than a lot that's never questioned.
  • Harta is asked about twice (Tirmidhi 2417): where it came from and where it went. Both sides need checking — clean sources, and spending that includes zakat and sedekah.
